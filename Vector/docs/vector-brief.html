Part 2 · How the first two stages actually think

1Finding exactly who to talk to

Every morning, Radar reads tech news the way a sharp researcher would, not to collect articles, but to spot the ones that mean "this company is about to spend money." A funding round, a hiring spree, a product launch. Each one it finds gets scored against your ideal customer profile, 0 to 100, with a plain-English reason attached, so nothing gets added to your pipeline without a reason you'd agree with.

Once a company clears the bar, Detective goes and finds the actual human to talk to, the founder, the CEO, the head of the department that would buy this, and doesn't stop at a name. It finds a real, verified email address, checked three different ways, so your first message doesn't bounce.

Example, start to finish
A headline drops: "Company X raises a $12M Series A to expand engineering." Radar reads it, tags it as a funding signal, and scores the company 82/100, Tier A, a strong signal and a strong fit. Detective then finds the CEO and a verified email, 95% confidence, in under a minute.
What Scout replaces

An SDR spending 2 hours a day scrolling news and LinkedIn, guessing at email addresses, and still missing the good ones. Scout does this for every company in your market, continuously, and never gets tired of page 4 of Google.

2Reaching out, without anyone dropping the ball

Pulse is the part that behaves like your best SDR, minus the human limits. It writes a personal email and a LinkedIn message for every single person, not a mail-merge, an actual draft written for that person and that signal. Then it follows up on schedule, automatically, for as long as it takes to get an answer.

Email and LinkedIn are run as one coordinated sequence, not two separate efforts. The email goes out first alongside a LinkedIn connection request; once that invite is accepted, Pulse folds LinkedIn into the same follow-up rhythm as the email thread, so a prospect never gets a duplicate touch or a message that ignores what already happened on the other channel. It also protects your reputation: new mailboxes start slow and ramp up gradually so you never look like a spam operation. It quietly tests a couple of versions of every message and keeps whichever one gets more replies. And when someone replies "interested," Pulse reads that reply, drafts the right response, and offers real open slots on your calendar, booking the meeting without another email in between.

Example, continuing the story
Day 0: personalized email and LinkedIn invite go out together. Day 3: a follow-up lands, and the invite gets accepted, so LinkedIn joins the same thread. The prospect replies "interested", Pulse answers, offers three time slots, and books a 30-minute intro call. The subject line that got the reply is now used for everyone else too.

Part 3 · Getting from meeting to closed deal

3Compass: a sales manager for every single call

The meeting is booked, and that's normally where the automation stops and the manual scramble begins: writing up notes, remembering to follow up, chasing a second meeting, guessing at pricing. Compass picks up right where Pulse leaves off, and does that scramble for you, the moment the call ends.

Next Steps Agent

Listens back through the call and turns it into a short, clear action list, who owes what, and by when, instead of you re-listening to a recording that evening.

Follow-up Booker

If the call ended with "let's continue this," it proposes real open times and books the next call itself, no "does Tuesday work?" email chain.

Proposal Insights

Pulls out what was actually said about budget, timeline and must-haves, so the proposal you send matches the conversation, not a generic price sheet.

Example, right after the call
A 30-minute call happens. Compass reads it and produces: a 3-item next-steps list (send the case study, loop in their technical lead, share security docs) assigned with due dates; a follow-up call booked for the following week since the prospect asked to "get the team in the room"; and a note flagging a Q3 budget of $40-60k straight onto the deal, so the proposal starts at the right number instead of a guess.
